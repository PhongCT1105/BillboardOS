IDEA
Imagine a marketing agency operating digital billboards in Times Square, San Francisco, and Seattle. Each billboard has an AI agent that understands its available advertising slots, estimates audience reach, and helps sell those slots to suitable companies.
The system would work through six core capabilities:
Understand the audience and location. Cameras and sensors estimate foot traffic and how long people stay nearby. Location and event information help estimate audience relevance—for example, a technology conference or a financial district. Profession should come from that context; a camera cannot reliably determine whether someone works in tech or finance.
Adjust prices dynamically. Prices respond to estimated audience reach, advertiser demand, available slots, time of day, and local events. A crowded sporting event could justify higher prices, while quieter periods trigger discounts to attract bookings. Seasonal patterns would depend on the location and observed demand.
Offer packages for different advertisers. Company size, campaign budget, and advertising goals inform the offer. A small startup might receive a discounted off-peak package, while a large company could purchase premium locations, peak hours, or a campaign across multiple billboards.
Understand each company’s campaign. Advertisers upload documents describing their business, product, target customers, preferred cities, budget, and advertising materials. The AI uses this information to recommend suitable billboards, schedules, and packages.
Find customers and manage communication. A prospecting agent identifies companies whose campaigns could match available inventory—for example, YC startups seeking exposure in San Francisco or financial businesses targeting New York. A communication channel handles inquiries, personalized proposals, negotiations, and follow-ups.
Manage bookings and financial operations. Once a deal is accepted, the system reserves the advertising slots, handles invoicing and payment tracking, schedules the ad, and records when it played. The owner sees bookings, revenue, available inventory, and audience estimates in one dashboard.
The optional agent-to-agent extension gives advertisers their own buying agents. An advertiser’s agent compares billboard offers and negotiates within its budget; the billboard’s agent seeks suitable bookings above the owner’s minimum price. After agreement, the buyer pays through an x402-enabled booking endpoint, and the campaign is scheduled. Your application implements the negotiation; x402 provides the payment mechanism.
